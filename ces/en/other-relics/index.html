Studies · Relics of the Passion

Other relics

Besides the Holy Shroud and the Holy Chalice, Christian tradition preserves other objects linked to the Passion. Several are in Spain and are studied alongside the Shroud.

  1. Home
  2. /Other relics

The Sudarium of Oviedo

Holy Chamber, Oviedo Cathedral

It is a linen cloth of about 84 × 53 cm, with no image, bearing stains of blood and fluid. Tradition identifies it with the cloth that covered Jesus' head, mentioned in the Gospel of John (Jn 20:7), and says it reached Oviedo from Jerusalem, by way of North Africa and Toledo, inside the Holy Ark.

It is shown to the faithful three days a year: Good Friday, 14 September (Feast of the Exaltation of the Cross) and 21 September. Its comparison with the Holy Shroud is one of the most active lines of research: some studies point to matches in the stains of the face, which other researchers dispute.

The Lignum Crucis of Liébana

Monastery of Santo Toribio de Liébana, Cantabria

Lignum Crucis means “wood of the cross”. Since antiquity, fragments of the cross that, according to tradition, Saint Helena found in Jerusalem have been distributed. The monastery of Santo Toribio keeps one that is considered the largest, set in a gilded silver cross.

Liébana is one of the holy places of Christendom with its own jubilee year, celebrated when 16 April, the feast of Saint Toribio, falls on a Sunday.

The Holy Face of Alicante

Monastery of the Holy Face, Alicante

A cloth bearing the face of Christ is venerated here; tradition links it to the veil of Veronica, the woman who, according to an ancient tradition, wiped Jesus' face on the way to Calvary. The relic reached Alicante in the 15th century.

Every spring, thousands of people walk from the city to the monastery in the Holy Face pilgrimage, one of the largest in the Valencia region.

The Crown of Thorns

Notre-Dame Cathedral, Paris

King Saint Louis of France acquired it in the 13th century and had the Sainte-Chapelle built to house it. What survives is a ring of braided rushes without thorns: over the centuries the thorns were distributed as separate relics.

During the 2019 Notre-Dame fire it was rescued by firefighters. It returned to the cathedral, now restored, in December 2024.

The Tunic of Argenteuil

Basilica of Saint-Denys, Argenteuil (France)

The Gospel of John says the soldiers cast lots for Jesus' tunic because it “was without seam, woven from the top throughout” (Jn 19:23). In Argenteuil, near Paris, a tunic that tradition identifies with it has been venerated since the Middle Ages.

As with other relics, studies of the fabric and its stains have produced results that are still debated. It is displayed on extraordinary occasions.
