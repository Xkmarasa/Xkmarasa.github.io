The Holy Chalice

Since 1437 Valencia Cathedral has venerated an agate cup that tradition identifies with the chalice of the Last Supper. It is one of the relics most closely tied to our city.

  1. Home
  2. /The Holy Chalice

The object

The Holy Chalice has three parts. The upper part, the cup, is made of agate and is the only one tradition regards as a relic. The base is another chalcedony cup turned upside down, and the two are joined by a knot and two gold handles with pearls and precious stones added in the Middle Ages.

In 1960 the archaeology professor Antonio Beltrán studied the piece and concluded that the upper cup comes from an eastern workshop and can be dated between the 2nd century BC and the 1st century AD. In other words, it is compatible with the time of Jesus; science cannot go further and confirm its use at the Last Supper.

From Rome to Huesca, according to tradition

Tradition says that Saint Peter took the chalice to Rome and that the first popes used it at Mass. In the 3rd century, during the persecution of Emperor Valerian, Pope Sixtus II is said to have entrusted it to his deacon, Saint Lawrence, a native of Huesca, who sent it to his homeland for safekeeping.

During the Muslim invasion the chalice is said to have been hidden in the Pyrenees, in several places, until it reached the monastery of San Juan de la Peña. This part of the story rests on tradition and late documents, and historians assess it in different ways.

The documented history

The first reliable document dates from 1399: the monks of San Juan de la Peña hand the chalice over to King Martin I of Aragon, the Humane, who keeps it in his Aljafería palace in Zaragoza and later in Barcelona.

His successor, Alfonso V the Magnanimous, moved it to the royal palace of Valencia and, in 1437, handed it over to the cathedral, where it has been kept ever since. During the Peninsular War and the Spanish Civil War it had to be taken out of the cathedral and hidden for protection, and in 1939 it returned to its chapel.

Veneration today

The Holy Chalice is displayed in the cathedral's former chapter house, now a chapel. Two popes celebrated Mass with it in Valencia: Saint John Paul II in 1982 and Benedict XVI in 2006, during the World Meeting of Families.

Since 2015 the Holy See has periodically granted Valencia Cathedral a Jubilee Year of the Holy Chalice, which draws pilgrims from all over the world. For the CES, the chalice is an essential part of the heritage we study and share from Valencia.

Key facts

Where it is
Chapel of the Holy Chalice, Valencia Cathedral
The cup
Eastern agate, about 9.5 cm in diameter
Dating of the cup
Between the 2nd century BC and the 1st century AD (1960 study)
Base and handles
Medieval goldsmith's work added later
In Valencia since
1437
