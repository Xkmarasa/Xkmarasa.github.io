Studies · The Passion

The sweat of blood

On the night of Holy Thursday, in the garden of Gethsemane, the Gospel of Luke says that Jesus' sweat became “like great drops of blood”. It is the first physical suffering of the Passion.

  1. Home
  2. /The sweat of blood

The account of Gethsemane

After the Last Supper, Jesus withdraws to pray in an olive grove across the Kidron valley. The Gospels describe his anguish at what is about to happen. Only Luke, whom tradition presents as a physician, adds a physical detail: “And being in agony he prayed more earnestly; and his sweat became like great drops of blood falling down upon the ground” (Lk 22:44).

These verses (43-44) are missing from some of the most important ancient manuscripts of the Gospel, while Christian writers of the 2nd century already knew them. Textual critics debate whether they belonged to the original text; the Church includes them in the canon of Scripture.

Haematidrosis in medicine

Medicine knows a very rare phenomenon, haematidrosis, in which a person sweats blood or blood-tinged sweat. It has been described in situations of extreme fear or anguish: the most widely accepted hypothesis is that the strain ruptures the small blood vessels around the sweat glands, and blood comes out mixed with sweat.

Several doctors who have studied the Passion point out that after such an episode the skin is left especially tender and fragile, which would have worsened the blows and the scourging that followed. These are medical readings of the text, not a diagnosis that can be verified.

Link with the relics of the Passion

Researchers who study the Holy Shroud and the Sudarium of Oviedo have analysed the bloodstains of the head and face to reconstruct the sequence of the Passion. Some interpret certain traces as compatible with bloody sweat; others consider it impossible to prove. The CES presents these lines of study with caution and always distinguishes between what the text says, what is observed and what is interpreted.

Gethsemane today

On the traditional site of the garden stands the Basilica of the Agony, also known as the Church of All Nations, next to a small grove of very old olive trees. Inside, the rock on which, according to tradition, Jesus prayed that night is venerated.
